How D-L86 is followed:

- **Columns.** One `listLeads` read per stage, `status=<that stage>` only, each paged inside its own
  column. No count on any chip, column or title.
- **Moves.** The only contact write is `{ version, status }`, to lead stages only, with the last-served
  version. A drop on the card's own column sends nothing.
  - Every card has a "Move" button (pointer and keyboard). The drag uses the platform's pointer events
    (mouse and pen), with no library.
  - A card moves when the server confirms, not optimistically. A refusal is a translated `role=alert`
    in the screen. A 409 re-reads the card with getContact and draws it where the server has it.
- **Convert.**
  - It is one `convertContact`, never createAccount and never listContacts.
  - The picker reads `listAccounts?q=` and filters to the lead's own `programmeId`.
  - It is not offered where `status` or `accountId` is withheld: the row shows a locked "Can't convert"
    instead.
  - A lost answer is its own alert, and the retry re-sends the same body (the mock's exact-repeat 200).
  - `already_linked` has its own sentence. A 409 re-reads the lead, so the next press is not blind.
- **Hidden:** Merge, Lead score, Map, bulk selection and bulk WhatsApp, Health. No UUID is printed. No
  device storage. Offline, every move, drag and convert is disabled and nothing queues.

## What I left out, and why

- **The column chooser.** P04/01 shows one, but nothing in the suite needs it, and the budget is tight.
  The shared `ColumnChooser` can be dropped in later. Its ids are bundled, because `list-parts.tsx`
  names them.
- **Programme names on the rows.** `/leads` makes no `listMyProgrammes` call: the only programme logic
  is the picker's client-side filter by id, which needs no names. A multi-programme BPO user cannot see
  which programme a lead is in. That is a cheap follow-up (T-0057's `useMyProgrammes`).
- **Touch drag.** On a touch screen the drag is off, so a finger scrolls the board. Touch users move a
  card with its "Move" button, which is the WCAG 2.5.7 alternative anyway.
- **The picker pages.** It reads at most 50 accounts for the term and filters by programme on the
  client. If the first 50 matches all belong to other programmes, nothing is offered. There is no
  "more" control, and the mock serves no cursor.
- **Convert from a Kanban card.** It is a table-row action only, per D-L86.
- **Cross-view state.** Switching between table and board re-reads, rather than carrying moves across.
  This is deliberate: it is always fresh, and the versions are always the server's.

## Open questions

1. **Reused `accounts.*` ids on the leads screen:** `accounts.sort.*`, `accounts.filter.locked`,
   `accounts.error.fieldWithheld`, `accounts.error.fieldNotEditable`, `accounts.error.notAccepted`,
   `accounts.invalid.name`, `accounts.sheet.close` and `accounts.sheet.cancel`. Their copy is generic,
   but the namespace is misleading. Should they move to `records.*`? That is a rename across both
   screens, best done after #104 merges.
2. **Hiding convert on converted leads.** A lead already `qualified` with an account shows "Converted"
   instead of a convert button. Re-converting would only be an exact repeat or an `already_linked`. Is
   that the product's intent, or should a converted lead offer convert to a different account? The
   contract says convert never moves a lead between accounts.
3. **The wordmark link.** On the records e2e process, the CRM wordmark now links `/leads`, because
   Leads comes before Contacts in the nav and the wordmark goes to the first live entry. Harmless, but
   worth knowing.

## Risks I am aware of

- **Tenancy.** No scope is ever sent. The convert picker's programme filter is a UX filter on ids the
  server already scoped, not a security boundary: the API still answers 404 for another programme's
  account.
- **Personal data.** Names, companies and numbers are drawn as text (`data-record-text`) and never
  parsed. Nothing reaches the device. The search term and cursors never enter the URL.
- **The drag** depends on `document.elementFromPoint` and inline `transform` and `pointer-events`
  styles on the lifted card, all reset on pointerup and pointercancel. A pointer lost mid-drag
  (`pointercancel`) drops nothing.
